Product and user testing

Free to start low effort online

Companies pay you to use their website or app while thinking aloud. Your screen and voice are recorded — your face usually is not.

How the money works

A company commissions usability tests. You complete tasks on their product while narrating, and are paid per accepted test.

Time to first payment

Around two weeks. UserTesting states payment typically arrives within 14 days of completing a test.

What you are actually being paid for

Not your opinion, and not your competence. Companies are buying evidence of confusion. Where you hesitate, misread a label, or click the wrong thing is the entire product.

This inverts the instinct most people have on camera. The temptation is to appear capable and move quickly. That produces a recording of no value. Narrating every moment of uncertainty — including the ones that feel embarrassing — is what makes a tester get invited back.

Why we do not quote a per-test rate

You will find plenty of sites confidently stating a figure per test. UserTesting’s own page does not: rewards vary by test type, duration and customer demand, and the amount for each specific test is shown on your dashboard before you accept it.

So the honest guidance is procedural rather than numerical: check the stated reward and the estimated duration on the dashboard, divide, and decide. You always have that information before committing.

Face exposure

Screen and voice are always recorded. Webcam is usually optional and disclosed per test. If you want to keep your face out of it, this works — but read each test’s requirements rather than assuming.

The realistic constraint

Invitation volume is demographic-matched and irregular. This is supplementary income by design. Anyone describing it as a reliable replacement for steady work is misrepresenting how allocation works.

How to start

  1. Check the technical requirements first. UserTesting specifies 3.0 Mbps upload, a supported browser, and 7 GB free disk space on desktop. Failing mid-test wastes your time and the reward.
  2. Take the qualification test seriously. Your first recorded test determines how many invitations you receive afterwards. It is effectively an audition.
  3. Practise narrating out loud. The product being sold is your commentary, not your task completion. Silent competence scores badly; confused-but-verbal scores well.
  4. Say what confuses you. Clients pay to find friction. 'I don't understand what this button does' is exactly the output they want.
  5. Respond to invitations immediately. Tests are allocated first-come and fill within minutes.

Scams and risks to know

Equipment purchase requirement

How it works: A 'testing company' requires you to buy specific software or hardware first, or sends an overpayment cheque to buy it.

How to avoid it: Real platforms work with equipment you already own. None require an up-front purchase.

Good fit if

  • People comfortable talking aloud while working
  • Anyone who wants no-cost online work without showing their face
  • Those with a reliable connection and flexible short blocks of time

Not for you if

  • Anyone uncomfortable being recorded
  • People needing predictable volume — invitations are demographic-matched and irregular
  • Those without a stable upload connection

Eligibility

  • A computer or smartphone meeting the platform's specs
  • A microphone
  • Willingness to be recorded speaking aloud
  • Comfortable narrating your thought process

Geography: International.
