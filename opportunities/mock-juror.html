Paid mock juror work

Free to start low effort online

Attorneys pay ordinary people to read real case summaries and deliver a verdict before trial. Free to join, and strictly limited by where you live.

How the money works

Trial attorneys test arguments before going to court. You review a case summary and answer verdict questions, and are paid per completed review. Payment reflects the estimated time required.

Time to first payment

Unpredictable — potentially months. You are waiting for a case in your specific jurisdiction, which is entirely outside your control.

A real market most people never hear about

Before a case goes to trial, attorneys want to know how ordinary people will react to their argument. Hiring a full jury consultancy is expensive, so online mock juror panels emerged as a cheaper way to test a case against people who resemble the actual jury pool.

You are not deciding anything. Nothing you submit affects a real verdict. You are a test audience for a legal argument.

The constraint that decides everything

You must live in the venue where the case will be tried. This is not a preference — it is the entire point, because the attorney is testing the case against the pool it will actually face.

The practical consequence: your earnings potential is set almost entirely by litigation activity near you, and there is nothing you can do to change it. Someone near a busy court sees regular invitations. Someone in a quiet jurisdiction may register and wait months.

Judge this opportunity by your postcode, not your effort.

What is verified, and what is not

OnlineVerdict’s own juror pages confirm: 18 or over, US citizen, resident in the trial venue, and no fees to sign up or participate. In-person mock trials are typically a full day and pay $200–$700 depending on length.

Payment for shorter online case reviews is widely reported elsewhere as $30–$60, but we could not confirm that on OnlineVerdict’s own pages, so treat it as unverified.

Register broadly

Because volume is jurisdiction-limited rather than effort-limited, registering with a single service usually produces very little. Several legitimate services operate in this space, and breadth is the only lever you actually control.

How to start

  1. Confirm you meet the hard requirements. 18+, US citizen, and resident in the trial venue. The venue requirement is absolute and is what determines your actual volume.
  2. Complete demographic registration accurately. Attorneys select panels to mirror a real jury pool. Your demographics are the matching criteria.
  3. Register with several services. Case flow at any single service is sporadic. Breadth is the only way to see meaningful volume.
  4. Write substantive reasoning. The verdict itself is less valuable than why you reached it. Thin answers reduce future invitations.
  5. Check equipment for mock trials. Virtual mock trials require a webcam and microphone on a desktop or laptop. Phones are not accepted.

Scams and risks to know

Paid 'juror registration'

How it works: A site charges a registration or training fee to access mock juror work.

How to avoid it: Legitimate services pay you and charge nothing. OnlineVerdict states there are no signup or participation fees.

Good fit if

  • People in or near active litigation venues
  • Anyone who reads carefully and writes clearly
  • Those wanting occasional well-compensated work rather than steady income

Not for you if

  • Non-US citizens — the citizenship requirement is absolute
  • Anyone needing predictable income
  • People in areas with little litigation activity

Eligibility

  • 18 or over
  • United States citizen
  • Resident in the venue where a given case will be tried
  • Able to read carefully and give reasoned written feedback
  • No conflict of interest with the parties involved

Geography: United States only, further restricted to the specific trial venue of each case.
