Renting your yard as a private dog park

$0–$300 low effort offline No face needed

Dog owners pay by the hour for private, fenced space where a reactive or anxious dog can run safely off-leash. Your ordinary back garden is the product.

How the money works

Guests book your space by the hour for private dog exercise. Sniffspot collects payment and deducts commission and card processing before paying you out.

Time to first payment

Days to weeks in an area with demand. Faster than storage rental because bookings are hourly and impulse-driven rather than contractual.

Why this market exists at all

It sounds absurd until you know the customer. A large share of guests have reactive dogs — animals that cannot safely be exercised in a public park because they lunge at other dogs. For those owners, public spaces are a source of dread. A private, empty, enclosed area is not a luxury; it is the only way their dog gets to run.

That is why the product is not really your grass. It is the guarantee of emptiness. Understand that and everything about how to list well follows.

What you actually get paid

Sniffspot’s commission is 22%, and card processing is 2.37% + $0.22 on US cards (3.87% + $0.22 on non-US cards). Sniffspot’s own worked example: a $10 booking nets the host about $7.34.

There are no up-front or monthly hosting fees, so listing costs nothing but time. If you are outside the US, Stripe adds $2.25 + 0.25% per payout, which matters a great deal if you withdraw frequently in small amounts — batch your payouts.

Fencing is not the gate you think it is

Unfenced spots are listed and used. They attract a different guest — someone with a recall-trained dog who wants space rather than containment. If your land is large and unfenced, describe it accurately rather than assuming you are ineligible.

What ends a hosting arrangement badly is not the absence of a fence. It is a fence described as secure that is not.

The honest risk

Dogs get injured, and dogs escape. Read the platform’s protection terms before your first booking rather than after an incident, and keep every booking on-platform so those terms actually apply.

How to start

  1. Check your fencing honestly. State the exact fence height and any gaps. Guests bring escape-prone dogs specifically because the space is private — a misdescribed fence is a lost dog.
  2. Guarantee genuine privacy. The entire product is that no other dog appears. If your yard is overlooked by a neighbour's unleashed dog, say so in the listing.
  3. Photograph the whole perimeter. Experienced guests examine fence lines in photos before booking. Show them rather than describing them.
  4. Set a buffer between bookings. Reactive-dog owners need certainty they will not meet the previous guest. A gap between slots is a selling point, not lost revenue.
  5. Price by the hour against local demand. Check what nearby spots charge. Hourly pricing means you can test rates quickly without a long commitment.

Scams and risks to know

Off-platform booking

How it works: A repeat guest suggests paying cash directly to avoid the platform cut.

How to avoid it: Decline. Off-platform bookings remove any insurance or dispute mechanism, and injuries on your property are your exposure.

Good fit if

  • Anyone with a private yard, especially a large or fenced one
  • Rural landowners with a field that earns nothing
  • People who want to test demand quickly without commitment

Not for you if

  • Shared or overlooked outdoor space
  • People with their own territorial dogs on site
  • Anyone who cannot guarantee the space is empty during bookings

Eligibility

  • You have a yard, field or enclosed land
  • You can guarantee no other dogs or people will be present during a booking
  • You have the right to host visitors on the property

Geography: Primarily United States. International hosts are supported but pay additional payout fees.
