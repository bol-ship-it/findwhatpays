Opportunities · Creator work

Faceless user-generated content

$0–$60 medium effort online

Brands pay for short product videos that look like a real customer filmed them. Many formats need only your hands, the product and a phone — no audience and no face.

How the money works

Brands order videos through a platform. You claim a job, receive the product, film to a brief, and are paid per accepted video. You are not paid for views — this is contract production work, not influencing.

Time to first payment

Weeks. Product shipping time sits between accepting a job and being able to film it.

This is production work, not influencing

The most useful thing to understand: you are not being paid for reach. You have no audience requirement, no follower threshold, and no algorithmic exposure. A brand is commissioning footage it will run as advertising on its own channels.

That reframing changes how to approach it. Reliability, brief compliance and turnaround speed are what get you rebooked. Charisma is close to irrelevant.

Why “faceless” is a genuine format, not a workaround

A large share of briefs are hands-only: unboxing, product demonstration, application, texture close-ups. These are not lesser jobs given to people who will not show their face. For many product categories they are the preferred format, because the product should be the subject.

If you want to earn from content without ever appearing, this is one of the few routes where that is the norm rather than a compromise.

The privacy limit to be clear about

You must receive physical products, which means giving brands or platforms a shipping address. You can stay off camera; you cannot stay unlocatable. If full anonymity is your requirement, this is the wrong opportunity and no workaround changes that.

Usage rights are the term that matters

A brief paying a modest per-video rate for organic social use is a different commercial proposition from the same rate granting unlimited perpetual paid-advertising rights.

Read the usage term on every job. It is the difference between selling a video and selling a campaign asset at video prices.

Why we do not quote rates

Per-video figures circulate widely and trace back to affiliate-funded blog posts rather than any published rate card. Rates are visible on the platform when you claim a job. Use those.

How to start

  1. Film by a window. Lighting separates accepted videos from rejected ones more than any other variable. Daylight is free.
  2. Shoot vertical and stabilise. Deliverables are almost always vertical. A cheap tripod removes the most common technical rejection.
  3. Follow the brief literally. Briefs specify hooks, shot counts and durations. Creative deviation is the most common cause of rejection, not poor production quality.
  4. Decide your face policy up front. Many briefs are hands-only. Filter for these deliberately if you want to stay off camera — it is a normal, well-supported format.
  5. Read the usage rights on every job. Whether footage runs as a paid advertisement changes what the work is worth.

Scams and risks to know

Gifting dressed as payment

How it works: A brand offers 'free product' as compensation for commercial video.

How to avoid it: Free product is not payment. Usage rights to commercial footage have real value — price them.

Unlimited perpetual usage rights

How it works: A low per-video fee grants unlimited paid advertising use in perpetuity.

How to avoid it: Check the usage term. Video used in paid ad campaigns is priced very differently from organic social use.

Portfolio-building unpaid work

How it works: An 'agency' commissions free videos to build your portfolio.

How to avoid it: Platforms with real paying briefs exist. Unpaid commercial production is not a necessary step.

Good fit if

  • People comfortable on camera with hands only
  • Anyone wanting creator income without building an audience
  • Those who can meet deadlines consistently

Not for you if

  • Anyone unwilling to receive physical products at their address
  • People who dislike prescriptive briefs and revision requests
  • Anyone expecting income from views

Eligibility

  • A smartphone that shoots decent video
  • Somewhere with reasonable natural light
  • A shipping address for products
  • Ability to meet deadlines reliably

Geography: Primarily United States.
