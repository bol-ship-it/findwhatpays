This is a genuinely different commercial situation, and the difference is worth understanding before you spend any time on it.

The structural difference

The buyer pool is substantially smaller. The seller pool is also substantially smaller. Those two facts do not cancel out into “about the same” — they change what actually determines whether you sell.

In the larger market, the problem is discovery: enormous competition means being found is the constraint. In this segment, the problem is demand density: less competition for marketplace placement, but far fewer buyers browsing to begin with.

What that means practically

Marketplace search alone is a weaker strategy here. Sellers in this segment who do earn typically bring buyers with them from elsewhere rather than waiting to be found on-platform.

If you are unwilling to do any outside promotion, the honest expectation is very little. That is not a discouragement so much as an accurate description of the mechanism — and it is the opposite of the advice in most content on this topic, which simply reuses general-market guidance.

Everything else is identical

The platforms are the same. The fee structures are the same. The privacy discipline is exactly the same and no less important — metadata stripping, no reused handles, no identifying detail in frame, everything on-platform, 18+ ID verification on the platform’s own domain.

Read the main feet-pictures entry for the full fee breakdown and privacy guidance. There is no separate set of rules for this segment, and any source presenting one is padding.

On earnings claims

How to start

  1. Strip metadata from every image. Phone photos embed EXIF data that can include precise GPS coordinates. This is the single most common way sellers are accidentally located. Remove metadata before uploading anything, anywhere.
  2. Remove identifying detail from the frame. Tattoos, scars, jewellery, distinctive flooring, house numbers, reflections in windows and visible mail all identify you. Check the whole frame, not the subject.
  3. Use a pseudonym and a dedicated email. Never reuse a username or email that appears anywhere else. A single reused handle links this activity to your real identity through a simple search.
  4. Verify age through the platform only. These platforms require 18+ and government ID verification. Upload ID only on the platform's own domain, typed directly into the address bar.
  5. Keep every conversation and payment on-platform. Off-platform contact removes your dispute recourse and is where extortion attempts begin, without exception.
  6. Watermark previews and reverse-image-search periodically. Assume anything public will be copied. Watermarking previews reduces resale value and searching for your own images tells you where they have spread.

Scams and risks to know

Fake buyer / advance-payment fraud

How it works: A 'buyer' sends a screenshot of a payment that never arrives, or overpays with a stolen instrument and asks for the difference back. The original payment is later reversed, leaving you liable.

How to avoid it: Only accept payment through the platform's own system. Never act on a screenshot, and never refund an overpayment directly.

Free-sample harvesting

How it works: A 'buyer' requests free samples to 'verify quality', then disappears and often resells the images.

How to avoid it: Legitimate buyers purchase preview content. Requests for free custom work are the most common time-waster in this market.

Content theft and resale

How it works: Images are scraped and resold, or posted to free aggregator sites.

How to avoid it: Watermark previews, keep resolution low on public content, and periodically reverse-image-search your own material.

Off-platform lure then extortion

How it works: A buyer pushes to move to a private messaging app, obtains identifying details, then threatens exposure to family or employer.

How to avoid it: Stay on-platform. Never share a real name, location, workplace, or a photo containing identifying background detail.

Fake 'agency' or manager

How it works: Someone offers to manage your account for a percentage, and requires account credentials or an up-front fee.

How to avoid it: Never hand over account credentials. Legitimate platforms do not require a manager.

Age-verification phishing

How it works: A fake 'verification' site harvests government ID images.

How to avoid it: Only ever upload ID through the platform's own verified domain, reached by typing the URL yourself.
