Opportunities · Digital assets

Selling simple digital templates and assets

$0–$20 medium effort online No face needed

Build a file once and sell it repeatedly — planners, spreadsheets, presets, resume templates. The only genuinely scalable thing in this guide, and the slowest to start.

How the money works

You create a digital file and list it. Buyers purchase and download it automatically. Marginal cost per additional sale is effectively zero, so revenue scales with demand rather than your hours.

Time to first payment

Weeks to months. Slow by design — you are building an asset, not completing a task.

The only genuinely scalable option here

Every other opportunity in this guide trades a finite resource: your hours, your space, your appearance. This one does not. A template that sells a hundred times costs no more to deliver than one that sells once.

The trade is that it is the slowest to produce a first payment, and the most likely to produce nothing at all.

The fee comparison that actually decides it

Etsy: $0.20 per listing (expiring after four months), 6.5% transaction fee on the displayed price, plus 3% + $0.25 payment processing. On a $10 digital sale that is roughly $1.20 in fees.

Gumroad: 10% + $0.50 per direct sale, no listing fee, no monthly fee. On the same $10 sale, $1.50. But sales made through Gumroad’s own Discover marketplace cost 30%.

On fees alone they are close. That is not the real difference.

Traffic is the real difference

Etsy has buyers already searching. Gumroad does not — it is a checkout, not a marketplace, unless you use Discover and pay 30% for the privilege.

So the question is not “which is cheaper” but “do I have an audience?” If you do, Gumroad keeps more of each sale. If you do not, Etsy’s $0.20 listing fee is buying you distribution, which is the expensive part.

Starting on Etsy and adding Gumroad later, once you have people who come to you directly, is a defensible sequence.

The failure mode

The overwhelming majority of digital template shops earn nothing, and the reason is almost always the same: a generic product in a saturated search term. “Budget spreadsheet” competes with thousands of listings. “Budget spreadsheet for freelancers with irregular income” competes with very few, and the person searching that phrase is far closer to buying.

Specificity is not a refinement here. It is the whole strategy.

How to start

  1. Solve a problem you personally have. Generic templates lose to volume sellers. Specific ones — a budget spreadsheet for freelancers with irregular income — have far less competition.
  2. Check demand before building. Search the marketplace for your idea. Zero results usually means no demand, not an untapped niche.
  3. Choose the platform for the traffic model. Etsy brings buyers but charges per listing and per sale. Gumroad charges less but sends you no traffic — you must bring your own.
  4. Write the listing for the buyer's problem. Buyers search by the problem, not the file format. The description does most of the selling.
  5. Make the preview do the work. Nobody buys a file they cannot see. Show the actual contents, not a mockup.

Scams and risks to know

Reselling licence traps

How it works: You buy a 'commercial licence' bundle and resell the templates, which the original licence does not actually permit.

How to avoid it: Read the licence. Most 'private label rights' bundles do not grant resale rights on marketplaces, and takedowns follow.

Course upsells

How it works: A 'passive income' course sells you the method rather than the market.

How to avoid it: Both platforms are free or near-free to test. Validate demand yourself before paying anyone to teach you.

Good fit if

  • People with an existing tool skill
  • Anyone wanting income that is not tied to hours worked
  • Those willing to wait months for compounding returns
  • People who want no face, no shipping and full pseudonymity

Not for you if

  • Anyone who needs money soon
  • People unwilling to iterate on things that do not sell
  • Those hoping to upload once and never revisit it

Eligibility

  • Competence in one design or document tool — Canva, Google Sheets, Notion and Figma all qualify
  • Willingness to produce something genuinely useful rather than generic
  • Patience: this compounds slowly

Geography: International, in platform-supported payout countries.
