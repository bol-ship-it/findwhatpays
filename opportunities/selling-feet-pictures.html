Selling feet pictures

A real, established market with dedicated verified marketplaces. Genuinely anonymous if you are careful, heavily oversupplied, and surrounded by more misinformation than anything else in this guide.

18+ only. The platforms covered here require you to be at least 18 and verify it with government photo ID. Only ever upload identity documents on the platform's own domain, reached by typing the address yourself.

How the money works

You list photo sets, videos or custom requests on a verified marketplace. Buyers purchase individually or subscribe to your profile. The platform handles payment, age verification and buyer anonymity, and takes a percentage.

Time to first payment

Days to weeks if you sell at all. Highly variable and demand-limited; many sellers never make a sale.

Start with the thing nobody tells you

This market is real, and it is heavily oversupplied.

Both halves matter. There genuinely are verified marketplaces, real buyers, and real payouts — this is not a myth. There is also a very large number of sellers competing for that demand, and the earnings figures circulating online are almost entirely produced by sites earning affiliate commission on your signup.

We publish no earnings estimate for this, and that is a deliberate choice. Every confident figure we found traced back to content with a financial interest in you signing up and no stated methodology. Treat any specific number you are shown as marketing until someone shows you how it was measured.

The fee facts, from primary sources

FeetFinder states that creators keep 90% of every sale, with weekly payouts direct to bank and no 30-day holds. Every creator completes mandatory government ID verification to confirm they are over 18. There is no exclusivity requirement, so you may sell elsewhere simultaneously.

You will find review sites stating FeetFinder charges a tiered 10–15% commission plus a $4.99–$14.99 monthly seller subscription. We could not confirm any seller subscription from FeetFinder’s own site or its Terms of Service — the only subscriptions the ToS describes are buyers subscribing to a seller’s profile. Verify pricing yourself at signup rather than trusting either account, including ours.

For Fun With Feet, we could not locate a public pricing or terms page. Secondary sources report $14.99 for a six-month seller subscription and 15% commission. We have not confirmed that, so we do not present it as fact.

Privacy is the actual skill

The money question is less important than this one, because a privacy mistake is not recoverable.

Metadata is the biggest single risk. Photos taken on a phone commonly embed GPS coordinates in EXIF data. Uploading an unstripped image can publish your home location. Strip metadata from every image before it goes anywhere.

The frame identifies you, not just the subject. Tattoos, scars, distinctive jewellery, recognisable flooring, a house number through a window, a reflection, mail on a table. Check the entire frame.

Never reuse a handle or email. One username shared with another account links this activity to your real identity in a single search. Use a dedicated pseudonym and a dedicated email address that exist nowhere else.

Stay on-platform. Every extortion pattern in this market begins with moving the conversation to a private messaging app. There is no legitimate reason a buyer needs that.

Accept that content spreads

Anything you publish may be copied and resold, and you will not be able to fully prevent it. Watermark previews, keep public resolution low, and reverse-image-search your own material occasionally so you know where it has gone.

If that possibility is genuinely unacceptable to you, this is the wrong opportunity. That is a legitimate conclusion and worth reaching before you upload rather than after.

Age verification is not optional

Every legitimate platform in this category requires you to be 18 or over and verifies it with government ID and a selfie. That requirement protects you as much as anyone. Upload identity documents only on the platform’s own domain, reached by typing the address yourself — fake verification pages harvesting ID scans are a known fraud pattern.

How to start

  1. Strip metadata from every image. Phone photos embed EXIF data that can include precise GPS coordinates. This is the single most common way sellers are accidentally located. Remove metadata before uploading anything, anywhere.
  2. Remove identifying detail from the frame. Tattoos, scars, jewellery, distinctive flooring, house numbers, reflections in windows and visible mail all identify you. Check the whole frame, not the subject.
  3. Use a pseudonym and a dedicated email. Never reuse a username or email that appears anywhere else. A single reused handle links this activity to your real identity through a simple search.
  4. Verify age through the platform only. These platforms require 18+ and government ID verification. Upload ID only on the platform's own domain, typed directly into the address bar.
  5. Keep every conversation and payment on-platform. Off-platform contact removes your dispute recourse and is where extortion attempts begin, without exception.
  6. Watermark previews and reverse-image-search periodically. Assume anything public will be copied. Watermarking previews reduces resale value and searching for your own images tells you where they have spread.

Scams and risks to know

Fake buyer / advance-payment fraud

How it works: A 'buyer' sends a screenshot of a payment that never arrives, or overpays with a stolen instrument and asks for the difference back. The original payment is later reversed, leaving you liable.

How to avoid it: Only accept payment through the platform's own system. Never act on a screenshot, and never refund an overpayment directly.

Free-sample harvesting

How it works: A 'buyer' requests free samples to 'verify quality', then disappears and often resells the images.

How to avoid it: Legitimate buyers purchase preview content. Requests for free custom work are the most common time-waster in this market.

Content theft and resale

How it works: Images are scraped and resold, or posted to free aggregator sites.

How to avoid it: Watermark previews, keep resolution low on public content, and periodically reverse-image-search your own material.

Off-platform lure then extortion

How it works: A buyer pushes to move to a private messaging app, obtains identifying details, then threatens exposure to family or employer.

How to avoid it: Stay on-platform. Never share a real name, location, workplace, or a photo containing identifying background detail.

Fake 'agency' or manager

How it works: Someone offers to manage your account for a percentage, and requires account credentials or an up-front fee.

How to avoid it: Never hand over account credentials. Legitimate platforms do not require a manager.

Age-verification phishing

How it works: A fake 'verification' site harvests government ID images.

How to avoid it: Only ever upload ID through the platform's own verified domain, reached by typing the URL yourself.

  • Those who can accept an oversupplied market and start without expectations

Not for you if

  • Anyone under 18 — no exceptions
  • People who cannot tolerate content being copied or resold
  • Anyone needing predictable income
  • People unwilling to complete ID verification

Eligibility

  • 18 or over — this is non-negotiable and ID-verified on legitimate platforms
  • Willing to complete government ID verification with the platform
  • A phone camera and decent lighting
  • Comfortable with the possibility that content is copied or resold

Geography: Available internationally on the major platforms.
