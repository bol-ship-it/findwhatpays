Scams in this market, and how each one works

The specific fraud patterns targeting sellers, what each one looks like from the inside, and the single rule that defeats most of them.

18+ only. The platforms discussed here require you to be at least 18 and to verify it with government photo ID. Only upload identity documents on a platform's own domain, reached by typing the address yourself.

One rule defeats most of this

Keep every conversation and every payment on the platform.

Almost every scam below requires moving you off-platform first, because that is where there are no records, no dispute process, and no consequences for the other party. If you internalise nothing else from this page, internalise that.

Payment fraud

The screenshot that isn’t a payment

A “buyer” sends a screenshot appearing to show a payment sent, then asks you to send content because “it’s processing”. No payment ever arrives.

Recognise it: any payment discussed outside the platform’s own system. Defeat it: treat only funds visible in your platform balance as real. Screenshots are trivially faked.

Overpayment and refund

A buyer “accidentally” sends more than agreed and asks you to return the difference. Their original payment is later reversed as fraudulent or charged back, leaving you out the refund you sent from your own money.

Recognise it: any overpayment followed by a refund request. There is no legitimate version. Defeat it: never refund an overpayment directly. Escalate to platform support.

The chargeback after delivery

A buyer pays with a stolen or reversible instrument, receives content, then disputes the charge.

Defeat it: use platforms that verify buyers and handle disputes. This is a concrete benefit of ID-verifying marketplaces over anonymous ones.

Content and identity fraud

Free-sample harvesting

A “buyer” requests free samples to “check quality” or “verify you’re real”, then disappears — often reselling what you sent.

Recognise it: any request for free custom content. Genuine buyers purchase preview content. Defeat it: never send free custom work. This is the most common time-waster in the market.

The off-platform lure, then extortion

The most serious pattern. A buyer is friendly and pays legitimately at first, then suggests moving to a private messaging app for “easier” contact. Over several conversations they collect identifying details — your town, your job, your first name, a background detail in a photo. Then they threaten to send your content to your family or employer unless you pay or send more.

Recognise it: any pressure to move off-platform, and any accumulating personal questions, however friendly. Defeat it: stay on-platform, disclose nothing identifying, and follow the anonymity guide. If it happens: do not pay. Paying reliably escalates rather than resolves it. Preserve the messages, report to the platform, and contact law enforcement — this is a crime in most jurisdictions, and you are the victim of it.

The fake “manager” or agency

Someone offers to grow your account for a percentage, and needs your login credentials or an up-front fee. They then take the account, the content, or the fee.

Recognise it: any request for account credentials, or payment before any service. Defeat it: never share credentials with anyone. These platforms do not require a manager.

Verification phishing

A message links to a “verification page” that harvests government ID images.

Recognise it: any verification request arriving via link, message or email. Defeat it: only ever verify on the platform’s own domain, typed into the address bar yourself. Never send ID to another user.

Platform-side risks that are not scams

Not every loss is fraud. Worth planning for:

  • Account suspension for a rule breach, sometimes with a balance held. Read the acceptable-use policy properly.
  • Payout delays. Understand the schedule before you rely on the money.
  • Content theft by other users, which no platform fully prevents. Watermark previews and reverse-image-search periodically.

The red flags, condensed

Treat any of these as reason to stop:

  • Wants to move off-platform
  • Requests free content of any kind
  • Offers to overpay
  • Asks personal questions, however casually
  • Sends a payment “screenshot”
  • Asks for your ID, real name, or location
  • Requests account credentials
  • Pressures you to decide quickly

Urgency is a tool. A legitimate buyer is fine with you taking your time and staying on-platform.
