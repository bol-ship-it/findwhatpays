Selling anonymously: an operational privacy guide

Concrete, checkable steps to sell without being identified. Privacy mistakes here are the ones you cannot undo, so this is the page to read before your first upload.

18+ only. The platforms discussed here require you to be at least 18 and to verify it with government photo ID. Only upload identity documents on a platform's own domain, reached by typing the address yourself.

Read this before you upload anything

Most privacy advice in this market is a vague instruction to “be careful”. This is a checklist you can actually execute.

The reason for the urgency: nearly every privacy failure here is irreversible. Content spreads, metadata gets indexed, and identities get linked permanently. Doing this correctly from the first upload is far easier than repairing it afterwards.

1. Strip metadata from every image

This is the highest-severity item on the page.

Photos taken on a phone commonly embed EXIF metadata, which can include precise GPS coordinates of where the photo was taken. Uploading an unstripped image can publish your home address in a form anyone can read.

Do this on every single image, every time:

  • On iPhone: share the photo and disable “All Photos Data” / location before exporting, or use a metadata-removal tool.
  • On Android: check the photo’s details and remove location, or use a metadata-removal tool.
  • Simplest reliable method: screenshot the photo and upload the screenshot. Screenshots do not carry the original’s EXIF data.

Do not assume a platform strips metadata. Some do; you cannot verify it and you do not need to rely on it.

2. Audit the whole frame, not the subject

Identification usually comes from the background rather than the subject. Before uploading, look for:

  • Tattoos, scars, birthmarks and distinctive jewellery — these are effectively biometric identifiers
  • Flooring, tiling, carpet and skirting — surprisingly distinctive when cross-referenced against a rental listing or a social post
  • Windows and mirrors — reflections capture rooms, faces and street views
  • Mail, packaging, prescription labels, name tags
  • House numbers or street signs visible outside
  • Recognisable bedding or furniture that also appears on your public social media

The reliable test: could someone who has been inside your home recognise the room?

3. Never reuse a username, email or photo

A single reused handle collapses the whole exercise. Search engines and specialised tools link accounts by username instantly.

  • Create a new pseudonym used nowhere else, ever.
  • Create a dedicated email address used only for this.
  • Never reuse a profile picture, banner or bio text from any other account, including cropped versions.
  • Do not link the account to any existing social profile.

4. Verify only on the platform’s real domain

Legitimate platforms require government photo ID to confirm you are 18 or over. That is a genuine safeguard.

But fake “verification” pages that harvest ID scans are a known fraud pattern, and a stolen ID is a serious problem.

  • Type the platform’s address yourself. Never reach verification through a link in an email or a message.
  • Check the domain carefully before uploading anything.
  • Never send ID to another user, under any circumstances. There is no legitimate reason for a buyer to see it.

5. Keep everything on-platform

Every extortion pattern in this market starts the same way: a buyer moves the conversation to a private messaging app, gathers identifying details over time, then threatens exposure to family or an employer.

  • Refuse to move to private messaging. There is no legitimate reason for it.
  • Never disclose your real name, town, workplace, school or schedule.
  • Be alert to friendly information-gathering conducted across several conversations.
  • Keep payment on-platform too. Off-platform payment removes all recourse.

6. Assume content will be copied

You cannot fully prevent this. You can reduce its value and know when it happens.

  • Watermark previews with your platform handle.
  • Keep public/preview images at low resolution; reserve full resolution for paid content.
  • Reverse-image-search your own content occasionally to see where it has spread.

7. Payment and tax leave a trail, by design

Being anonymous to buyers is achievable. Being anonymous to the platform, your bank, or the tax authority is not, and should not be your goal — it is what makes payment and dispute resolution work.

Income from this is generally taxable like any other income. Plan for that from the start rather than discovering it later.

The realistic summary

Anonymity from buyers: achievable, with the discipline above. Anonymity from the platform and payment system: not achievable, and not desirable.

If your requirement is that literally no entity anywhere can connect this activity to you, that requirement cannot be met, and it is better to know that before you begin than after.
