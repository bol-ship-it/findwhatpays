Where to sell feet pics: how to choose a platform

A decision framework rather than a ranked list. The platform choice matters far less than most guides claim, and the criteria that actually matter are not the ones usually compared.

18+ only. The platforms discussed here require you to be at least 18 and to verify it with government photo ID. Only upload identity documents on a platform's own domain, reached by typing the address yourself.

The uncomfortable answer first

Platform choice is not the main determinant of whether you sell anything. Demand is, and demand does not change much between marketplaces.

Almost every guide on this topic is structured as a ranked list of platforms, because ranked lists convert well into affiliate signups. That structure implies your outcome depends mostly on picking the right site. It mostly does not.

So this page is a framework for eliminating bad options, not a leaderboard.

The four criteria that actually matter

1. Does it verify age and identity?

This is the single most important filter, and it protects you in two directions.

A platform requiring government ID from everyone keeps minors out of a market they must not be in, and it substantially reduces fraud and chargebacks, because buyers are traceable.

FeetFinder requires every creator to complete government photo ID plus selfie verification. If a platform lets anyone list content anonymously with no verification at all, that is not a privacy feature — it is an indication of who else is on the platform and how much recourse you will have.

2. Can you stay anonymous from buyers while being verified to the platform?

These are different things, and conflating them is a common error.

You want the platform to know who you are — that is what makes payment and dispute resolution work. You do not want buyers to. FeetFinder’s model separates these: ID verification is used for age and identity checks, while creators trade under a pseudonym.

Any arrangement requiring you to reveal identifying details to a buyer should be rejected outright.

3. What does it actually cost, and is that cost knowable up front?

Compare the total cost of selling, not the headline percentage. That means commission plus any seller subscription plus payment processing.

FeetFinder states creators keep 90% of every sale, with weekly payouts direct to bank. For Fun With Feet, we could not locate a public pricing page at all, which is itself a meaningful data point: you should be able to find out what a platform charges before you sign up.

See FeetFinder fees for the full breakdown and a correction of some widely-repeated misinformation.

4. Does it hold payments, or are you on your own?

Marketplaces that process payment and hold funds give you recourse when a buyer disputes. Classifieds-style sites that simply host a listing do not, which shifts all fraud risk onto you.

On “FeetFinder alternatives”

People search for alternatives for two different reasons, and they need different answers.

“I want a different platform because I was rejected or banned.” Verification exists for a reason and a second platform will usually apply similar checks. If the rejection was for age or identity, no alternative solves that.

“I want to sell in more than one place.” This is reasonable and generally sensible. FeetFinder imposes no exclusivity requirement, so you can list simultaneously elsewhere. Because the constraint on your earnings is buyer demand rather than platform capacity, being present in more than one marketplace is one of the few genuine levers you have.

We have not created a separate “alternatives” page, because it would repeat this section with different words purely to capture a search phrase. The honest answer is: apply the four criteria above to any platform you are considering, including the ones we cover.

What we deliberately do not tell you

Which platform pays more. We have no reliable data on comparative earnings, and neither does anyone presenting you with a ranked table of expected income. Those figures are marketing.

Before you list anywhere

Read selling anonymously first. Privacy mistakes are the ones you cannot undo, and they are much easier to avoid before your first upload than to repair afterwards.
