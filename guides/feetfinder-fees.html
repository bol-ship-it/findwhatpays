FeetFinder fees: what the platform actually says

What FeetFinder's own site and Terms of Service state about fees and payouts, and why several widely-repeated figures do not match those documents.

18+ only. The platforms discussed here require you to be at least 18 and to verify it with government photo ID. Only upload identity documents on a platform's own domain, reached by typing the address yourself.

What FeetFinder’s own documentation says

Verified against FeetFinder’s own site and Terms of Service on 12 August 2026:

ItemWhat the platform states
Creator share90% of every sale
Payout frequencyWeekly, direct to bank account
Payout holdsNone — the platform states there are no 30-day holds
Age requirement18+, enforced by ID verification
VerificationGovernment photo ID plus a selfie holding it
ExclusivityNone — you may sell on other platforms simultaneously

Where other sources disagree, and what to make of it

Numerous review sites state that FeetFinder charges sellers a tiered 10–15% commission depending on plan, plus a $4.99–$14.99 per month seller subscription.

We could not confirm any seller subscription from FeetFinder’s own materials. Two specific checks:

  1. FeetFinder’s own site consistently states creators keep 90% of every sale, and describes this as having no hidden cut.
  2. FeetFinder’s Terms of Service does discuss subscriptions at length — but every reference describes a buyer subscribing to a seller’s profile, which is a product feature you can offer buyers, not a fee you pay.

That is not the same as proving no seller plan exists. A paid tier could exist behind the signup flow, which we have not gone through. What we can say precisely is: the published documentation does not support the figures those sites quote, and the sites quoting them earn commission on signups.

What to actually do

Treat this page as a starting point, not a final answer, and verify the fee schedule during signup before you upload anything or pay anything. That advice applies to our figures as much as anyone else’s.

If you find a current seller plan that contradicts what is here, the correct response is for this page to be corrected.

The fee is not the important number

A 10% commission versus a 15% commission changes your net by five cents on a dollar. Whether anyone buys at all changes it by a dollar.

Fee comparison is where affiliate-driven content concentrates, because it produces neat tables and confident recommendations. It is genuinely the least important variable in this decision. Demand, your privacy discipline, and whether the platform verifies its users all matter more.

What we could not verify

  • Whether a paid seller plan exists, and its price if so.
  • The minimum payout threshold.

Both should be visible during signup. Neither is a reason to avoid the platform; both are reasons to read the screen rather than trust a blog.
